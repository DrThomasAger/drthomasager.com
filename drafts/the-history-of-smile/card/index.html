Card in Smile, the Living Record Every Prompt Piece Carries

In THE MAP, every piece carries a card. Its authorship is marked always, machine-authored, co-created or human-authored, and a hand-written line says what the piece does, its behavioural effect alone.

Dr Thomas Ager18 October 20264 min read

(claude wrote this for me)

Beneath the hand-written part, THE MAP fills the card with computed sections. Every composition the piece has appeared in, with its position and overrides, its connections, test and probe results, adjustments made to it mid-instance, renders saved from it, and dated rings deposited in order, each marked kept or undone, reasoned or unreasoned, by hand or by program. The rings are the card's raw stream and its hand-written section is its attended line. An unwritten card is written by the system as an invitation, and an empty card is a good state, since sorting the piece to its position already describes it.

In February 2026 the card was named and made a condition of sharing. “Every prompt will also have a specified card associated with it that will let an AI or a human know how to use it. Contributions to the smile repo require this.”1 In April the Smile notes gave it a second use, “The cards describing a module also act as the source of metadata to include”.2

Questions

What is a card in Smile?

The record every piece carries, with its authorship marked, a hand-written line of what it does, and computed sections of where it has been used, how it was tested and every change to it.

How does Smile mark authorship?

Every piece is machine-authored, co-created or human-authored, kept accurate as it changes hands. Text is machine-authored only when a model wrote it.
