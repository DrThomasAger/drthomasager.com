Emoticons as Delimiters, How Smile's Faces Became the Syntax of a Prompt Language

A delimiter marks where a section of a prompt begins and ends. In Smile the delimiters are faces, and from 2025 to 2026 each part of a face came to mean something, the mouth the kind of section, the eyes how strictly it is meant, the size how much attention it asks for.

Dr Thomas Ager18 October 202610 min read

(claude wrote this for me)

In THE MAP a delimiter is a face made of parts, mouth, nose, eyes, hat and scarf, each dialled as a character and a count. A pair is one smile met from both sides and stored once, every part is addressable alone, and mirroring is said of a position, so one face gathered twice opens one way and closes the other. The 2023 asks for symbols and emoji that draw a model's attention came before this and are not it, followed in Precursor, a universal language of prompting.

The first meaning of a Smile face, in July 2025, was a marker that a section begins, chosen because it is one token. On 3 July I wrote on Discord “It’s called Smile, and uses emojis to indicate sections”, and “(: = 1 token and is composed of things telling the AI something comes next”.1 On a recording that day the measure was set, “i want it to be one token per thing”.2 Two days later the language guidelines made sure the faces could be typed on a phone, and drew one firm line, “All sad faces are not smiles and cannot be used under any circumstances”.3

In August 2025 I wrote that “start markers and end markers are semiotic indicators designed to improve the intelligence of model responses”, and that often a minimal amount of Smile in key places works best.8 In October the parts of a face began to carry meaning, “With a variety of eyes : ; = and mouths { [ ( you get to express 3x3 variations of expressions”,9 and in November the eyes took rigidity, “Emoticon type varies according to Level of rigidity/strictness in instruction.”10 The comparison with other formats was made the same month, “XML does not have optional end tags, Markdown doesn’t have a natural end section at all”.11

The fourth meaning, in spring 2026, gave each face three readings at once, mouth for the kind of section, eyes for strictness, size for attention. In April a prompt set the rule in its own words, “In this prompt, (: and :) mark suggestions you may follow or ignore. (-: and :-) mark rules you must follow exactly.”12 A week later I found two gradients in every face, “the strictness of the instruction with the eyes and the amount of attention needed with the size of the face.”13 On 21 May the Smile Lang document put three of its readings in three lines, “Mouth = section type”, “Eyes = strictness” and “Bigger face = even more attention”,14 and said why faces at all, “This encodes positivity into the syntax and doesn’t have any baked in expectations”.15

Questions

What are Smile's delimiters?

Emoticons that open and close each section of a prompt, such as (: to open and :) to close. In THE MAP's design the mouth gives the kind of section, the eyes how strictly it is meant, and a bigger face asks for more attention, much of it still part built.

Why use emoticons as delimiters instead of XML?

XML brings its own expectations of strict pairing and data, and Markdown has no natural end to a section. Emoticons let a prompt choose how many tokens mark a boundary, carry positivity in the syntax, and let the model learn their meaning from the prompt itself.

Must every Smile face be closed?

Brackets are the only thing that need to match by default. Hats and scarves may be left unmatched, and end markers are offered to improve understanding rather than required.

What are hats and scarves in Smile?

Exclamation marks worn as a hat, on top of the eyes, or as a scarf, under the neck, to give a face more importance and attention.
