Archetypal Smile, the Answer to Whether Something Can Be Smile

In THE MAP, Archetypal Smile answers one question, can this be Smile? It keeps every face that can be Smile. Meaning belongs to a version, bounds belong to a version, and use belongs to a lens.

Dr Thomas Ager18 October 20264 min read

(claude wrote this for me)

THE MAP gives the archetype its parts, mouths, eyes, noses, and emphasis worn as hats and scarves, in an anatomical order that makes marks read as a face. Every face smiles, true in every version, and a face that frowns is recognised and refused by name. The mouth carries the smile, while noses and eyes are neutral on their own. Orientation is its own dimension, forward or mirrored, and a mirrored smile closes the closest face before it that matches its nesting. Greater size means more, and each mark brings the meaning it already has in ordinary writing. The archetype grows when a version needs something it cannot yet express.

The word archetypal is in my words of October 2023, a search for the archetypal patterns of prompting, followed in Precursor, the archetypal patterns of prompting.

In July 2025 the faces arrived and were spoken of as beings, “how I use entities I'm like they're memorable interesting semantic characters that help you structure your prompt”.1 Their parts began to loosen, “For a long time I thought that you needed the kolons, the eyes, but you don't.”2 and “Smiles can have only eyes, only mouths, or both.”3

Questions

What is Archetypal Smile?

The layer of Smile that answers whether something can be Smile, keeping every face that smiles with its parts and their order, while each version gives particular faces their meanings and bounds.

What makes a face a smile?

The mouth. Noses and eyes are neutral on their own, and the mouth's turn relative to the eyes is what smiles.
