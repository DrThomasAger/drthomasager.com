Composition in the Smile Prompt Language, an Ordered Set of References Everything Is Made Of

In THE MAP, a composition is an ordered set of references, to raw text and to other compositions, nested without limit. Everything in the system is raw text or composition, and module, section, header, face and prompt are names a lens gives to positions.

Dr Thomas Ager18 October 20266 min read

(claude wrote this for me)

THE MAP gives a composition its settings, what it gathers and in what order, what is joined between the parts, which gathered parts are mirrored to close what they opened, the value of each variable, which lenses it may draw from, and its whitespace. An outer composition overrides an inner one, every override is stored as a new raw text so the library grows out of use, and rendering a composition is compiling it while decomposing is decompiling. A composition that sends is an emergence, the same kind with one difference.

The wish behind it is older than the word. In October 2023 I asked GPT-4 to break any prompt into its composable pieces and to separate structure from content, a precursor followed in Precursor, the archetypal patterns of prompting, before Smile had its faces or its system.

The first composer came in November 2025, with its own names for the parts. On 6 November, building a module from files, “You need to not rewrite any of the text I gave you.”1 The same day I reached for living tissue, “A composition of multiple cells is called tissue.”2 On 19 November the editor had to nest them, “I must be able to create compositions inside of compositions and all adding fucntionality must work.”3

Questions

What is a composition in Smile?

An ordered set of references to raw text and other compositions, nested without limit, with settings for what it gathers, how parts are joined and mirrored, variables and overrides. Everything in Smile is raw text or a composition.

What is the difference between rendering and decomposing?

Rendering a composition is compiling it into text, and decomposing is decompiling, carrying any text back into a set of compositions.
