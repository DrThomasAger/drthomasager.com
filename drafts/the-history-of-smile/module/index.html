Module in the Smile Prompt Language, a Piece of Writing With a Header, a Body and a Footer

In THE MAP, a module is a piece of writing at a position, or smaller pieces gathered, with the three-region form. A header and a footer each have a prefix, a start face, a label, an end face and a postfix, and the body has a header, body and footer of its own.

Dr Thomas Ager18 October 20269 min read

(claude wrote this for me)

THE MAP makes every three-region set optional in any combination, so whichever regions are filled are what the module is. Every part may be empty and carries its own newline setting, any of the four line positions may be turned off, and formatting is kept exactly as written or indented by the assembly. A module renders on its own with its defaults, so it can be seen whole before any composition gathers it. A lens places it, and defaults derive from where it is placed, so what a module carries is a preference a composition may replace. It can carry variables and their defaults, and sending, off by default, lets one module answer through a model of its own. Module is one of the names a lens gives to a position in a Composition.

Questions

What is a module in Smile?

A piece of writing at a position with a header, a body and a footer. The header and footer each have a prefix, a start face, a label, an end face and a postfix, and every region is optional.

Can a module be rendered on its own?

Yes. A module renders with its defaults, including its default header, so it can be seen whole before any composition gathers it.

How is a prompt turned into modules?

It is decomposed, each semantically distinct piece becoming a module of raw text with its header and footer, so the pieces can be reused, compared and optimised separately.
