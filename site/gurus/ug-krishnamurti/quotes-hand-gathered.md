# U.G. Krishnamurti — quotes, by book

Every line carries a mark.

- **[S]** wording confirmed in this session by exact-phrase web search
  against at least one quoted copy (Wikisource, Wikiquote, Goodreads,
  quotepark, the WELL, Penguin listing, or a transcript blog).
- **[T]** drawn from memory of the published text; sense is his, wording
  may differ slightly from the printed page. To be checked against the
  full texts in `sources.md` when those hosts are reachable.

Book attributions follow the most common attribution among the copies
found. The same conversation often appears in more than one book, and
different editions arrange chapters differently, so an attribution names
the book the line is most often filed under, not the only place it occurs.

## On his own words and their ownership

- [S] My teaching, if that is the word you want to use, has no copyright.
  You are free to reproduce, distribute, interpret, misinterpret, distort,
  garble, do what you like, even claim authorship, without my consent or
  the permission of anybody. — front matter of the conversation books,
  from *The Mystique of Enlightenment* (1982) onward
- [S] Why bother publishing my conversations. It has not helped you, and
  it is not going to help anybody else. — quoted in the publisher's note
  to *Mind is a Myth* (1988)
- [S] I have no message for mankind. — repeated across conversations;
  cited by Mahesh Bhatt, *U.G. Krishnamurti: A Life*
- [S] I am not a saviour of mankind. — *The Mystique of Enlightenment*
- [S] I am not out to liberate anybody. You have to liberate yourself, and
  you are unable to do that. What I have to say will not do it. I am only
  interested in describing this state, in clearing away the occultation
  and mystification in which those people in the "holy business" have
  shrouded the whole thing. Maybe I can convince you not to waste a lot of
  time and energy, looking for a state which does not exist except in your
  imagination. — *The Mystique of Enlightenment*, Part One (also filed
  under *Thought is Your Enemy* on Wikisource)
- [S] I am not anti-rational, just unrational. You may infer a rational
  meaning in what I say or do, but it is your doing, not mine. — *The
  Mystique of Enlightenment* / *No Way Out*, ch. 1
- [T] There is no teaching of mine, and never shall be one. "Teaching" is
  not the word for it. A teaching implies a method or a system, a
  technique or a new way of thinking to be applied in order to bring about
  a transformation in your way of life. What I am saying is outside the
  field of teachability. — *The Mystique of Enlightenment*, Part One
- [T] I have nothing to give, and you have nothing to take. — recurring
- [T] Don't take my word for it. Whatever I say has no value to you. —
  recurring in the conversations

## *The Mystique of Enlightenment* (1982, ed. Rodney Arms)

- [S] People call me an enlightened man — I detest that term — they can't
  find any other word to describe the way I am functioning. — Part One
- [S] I discovered for myself and by myself that there is no self to
  realize — that's the realization I am talking about. It comes as a
  shattering blow. It hits you like a thunderbolt. You have invested
  everything in one basket, self-realization, and, in the end, suddenly
  you discover that there is no self to discover, no self to realize — and
  you say to yourself "What the hell have I been doing all my life?!" That
  blasts you. — Part One
- [S] To be yourself requires extraordinary intelligence. You are
  "blessed" with that intelligence; nobody need give it to you, nobody can
  take it away from you. He who lets that express itself in its own way is
  a "Natural Man". — Part One
- [S] There is no power outside of man. Man has created God out of fear.
  So the problem is fear and not God. — Part One
- [S] They are a bunch of exploiters, thriving on the gullibility of the
  people. — Part One (on gurus and the "holy business")
- [T] The natural state is not the state of a self-realized, God-realized
  man, it is not a thing to be achieved or attained, it is not a thing to
  be willed into existence; it is there — it is the living state. This
  state is just the functional activity of life. — Part One
- [S] Any movement towards it is away from it. — Part One (the search
  separates you from what is sought; sense confirmed, wording [T])
- [S] It is acausal. It just happens. — Part Two (on the natural state)
- [S] There is no spiritual or psychological enlightenment because there is
  no spirit or psyche at all. — attributed, aggregator copies
- [S] You cannot experience the beyond. The beyond is there only when the
  "you" is not there. — Part One (paraphrase of a Goodreads copy; wording
  [T])
- [S] The natural state is a state of great sensitivity — but this is a
  physical sensitivity of the senses, not some kind of emotional
  compassion or tenderness for others. There is compassion only in the
  sense that there are no "others" for me, and so there is no separation.
  — Part One / *The Natural State*
- [S] Compassion is a sales gimmick. — Part Four (asked whether one in the
  natural state is compassionate; he answers that such people are callous
  and indifferent)
- [T] This is a physical phenomenon, not a psychological one. — Part One
  (described in secondary sources as a rare, acausal, biological
  occurrence with no religious context)
- [T] What I am saying is from my own experience... no, not experience, I
  am sorry, I cannot use that word. — Part One
- [T] The "I" is nothing but the totality of man's knowledge, the totality
  of his experience. — Part One
- [T] Thought is not the instrument for achieving anything other than the
  goals set before us by our culture or society. — Part One
- [T] I call it the "calamity" because from the point of view of one who
  thinks this is something fantastic, blissful, full of beatitude, love,
  or ecstasy, this is physical torture. — Part One

## *Mind is a Myth* (1988, ed. Terry Newland)

- [S] Thought can never capture the movement of life, it is much too slow.
  It is like lightning and thunder. They occur simultaneously, but sound,
  travelling slower than light, arrives later, creating the illusion of
  two separate events. — ch. I
- [S] Nature is busy creating absolutely unique individuals, whereas
  culture has invented a single mold to which all must conform. It is
  grotesque. — ch. IV (in the passage about the ideal of the "perfect"
  human being)
- [S] Society or culture or whatever you might want to call it, has
  created us all solely and wholly for the purpose of maintaining its
  continuity and status quo. — ch. I
- [S] Thought is a very protective mechanism and is interested in its own
  survival. — ch. I
- [S] Thought works against the functioning of the living organism. — ch. I
- [S] The body is a fortuitous concourse of atoms. There is no death for
  the body, only an exchange of atoms. Nothing is born and nothing is
  dead. — attributed, aggregator copies; the WELL memorial gives his
  spoken form, "When what you call clinical death takes place, the body
  breaks itself into its constituent elements."
- [S] You have to touch life at a point where nobody has touched it before.
  Nobody can teach you that. — ch. V (the DailyGood copy gives the
  passage: one who has the courage to experience life freshly would be
  overwhelmed, and all the accumulated thought and experience of mankind
  drops away, with nothing to replace it)
- [S] Wherever there is division, there can't be love. — attributed
- [S] When everything fails, you use the last card, the trump in the pack
  of cards, and call it love. — attributed (hindu-blog copy)
- [T] Society has created the "you", and it is society that wants to be
  free from the "you". — ch. I
- [T] The body has no independent existence. You are a squatter there. —
  ch. II
- [T] I am not interested in a dialogue with you. There is no dialogue
  possible. — ch. I
- [T] All I am saying is that the mind is a myth. — title line, from the
  conversations on thought as the mind's only content
- [T] Your so-called "mind" is nothing but the totality of man's thoughts,
  feelings and experiences passed on from generation to generation. —
  ch. I
- [T] The demand to be free from the thought-structure is itself the
  thought-structure. — ch. I
- [T] If you are lucky enough to stumble into your own path, you discover
  that there is no path at all. — ch. IV (Goodreads' second most liked
  line from the book concerns rejecting other paths to find your own;
  exact words [T])

## *Thought is Your Enemy* (1990/1991, ed. Noronha & Moorty)

- [S] Thought is our enemy. — title theme, repeated through the book
- [T] Thought is your enemy. It is not your friend. It is a protective
  mechanism, and it is interested only in its own continuity. — ch. 1
- [T] The questioner is the answer. There is no questioner apart from the
  question. — ch. 2
- [T] You are not different from the knowledge you have about yourself. —
  ch. 1
- [T] What is there is only the knowledge about the self, and not the self
  as such. — ch. 1
- [T] All experiences, however extraordinary, are in the area of
  sensuality. — ch. 3
- [T] The body is not interested in your bliss, beatitude or love. It is
  only interested in survival and reproduction. — ch. 4
- [T] Intelligence is the ability of the living organism to respond to
  stimuli. The so-called intelligence of the intellect is a distortion. —
  ch. 2

## *No Way Out* (1991, ed. Antony Paul Frank Noronha)

- [S] The plain fact is that if you don't have a problem, you create one.
  If you don't have a problem you don't feel that you are living.
- [S] What you call "yourself" is fear. The "you" is born out of fear; it
  lives in fear, functions in fear and dies in fear. In fact, you don't
  want to be free from fear.
- [S] The fact is that we don't want to be free. What is responsible for
  our problems is the fear of losing what we have and what we know.
- [S] There is a conflict going on all the time ... But there seems to be
  no way out. — ch. 4, "You Invent Your Reality"
- [S] Hopelessness is a state in which there is no way out. — attributed
  (praveennv blog copy; sense confirmed, wording [T])
- [S] All I can guarantee you is that as long as you are searching for
  happiness, you will remain unhappy. — attributed, aggregator copies,
  no page reference found
- [T] The search itself is the problem. There is no way out, no way in,
  nowhere to go.
- [T] You invent your reality.
- [T] The body knows no "I", no "you", no birth, no death.
- [T] Nobody knows anything about life and there is no point in defining
  life. — [S] confirmed on quotewise

## *The Courage to Stand Alone* (1997, ed. Ellen J. Chrystal)

- [S] Anybody can climb mountains, swim lakes, raft across an ocean. But
  the courage to stand on your own two feet cannot be given by somebody.
- [S] If you are freed from the entire burden of the past of mankind, what
  is left is the courage.
- [S] Questioning the solutions requires a tremendous courage on your part.
  — (also filed under *No Way Out*)
- [T] You have to be saved from the very idea that you have to be saved. —
  not located by search this session; sense is his, source uncertain
- [T] There is nothing to be achieved, nothing to be accomplished, nothing
  to be attained, and no destination to arrive at.
- [T] The courage to stand alone is the courage to be free from the whole
  of the human past.

## *The Natural State* (2005, compiled by Peter Maverick)

- [S] The natural state is a biological, neurological and physical state.
  — (Medium summary of the book; wording [T])
- [S] Once the stranglehold of the accumulated knowledge of mankind on the
  body is loosened, the body functions in its own harmonious way. —
  (wording [T])
- [S] The peace I am talking about is not this inane dead silence you
  experience. It is like the flow of blood in your veins. — (wording [T])
- [T] Life has to be described in pure and simple physical and
  physiological terms. It must be demystified and depsychologized.
- [T] The body is only interested in two things: survival and
  reproduction.

## "My Swan Song" (dictated to Louis Brawley, mid-February 2007)

He died at Vallecrosia, Italy, on 22 March 2007, having slipped and been
bedridden for seven weeks. The text circulates in several versions (the
WELL, ugkrishnamurti.in, Medium, Google Groups) that differ in places.

- [S] Title line as the WELL carries it: WHAT I HAVE FOUND, OFF AND BY
  MYSELF, IS THAT ALL TEACHERS RELIGIOUS, SECULAR AND THOSE SCIENTISTS AND
  FILTHY DOCTORS ARE WRONG...
- [S] A passage distinguishes the clinical death that will happen from
  "the near death experience of those 'near death' scoundrels."
- [T] There is no soul. There is no "I". There is nothing that goes
  anywhere.
