Preface

Uppaluri Gopala Krishnamurti was born in 1918 in Masulipatnam, in what is now Andhra Pradesh, and was raised by a grandfather devoted to the Theosophical Society. He spent his youth inside the religious life of his time. He practised, studied, lectured, met teachers, and came away from all of it with the sense that none of them had what they promised. In 1967, at the age of forty-nine, in Saanen in Switzerland, something happened to him that he never called enlightenment. He called it the calamity. For about a week his body went through changes he described in physical detail, and afterwards he lived in what he named the natural state, a functioning of the senses and the organism without a centre that interprets.

He spent the forty years that followed talking with whoever came to see him, in living rooms and kitchens, in India, Europe, America and Australia. He kept no organisation, gave no method, accepted no disciples, and said again and again that his state could not be passed on, imitated or reached. He released his words from copyright, which is why a book like this one can be made almost entirely of them. He died in 2007 in Vallecrosia, in Italy.

This book gathers what he said about thought, the body and the self. It does not try to make him gentle. He held that thought is a self-protective mechanism and the very thing that creates the self it protects. He held that the body is intelligent and needs nothing from religion or philosophy. He held that the search for liberation is the obstacle to the state it imagines. He refused to be a guru, and he meant it.

Every passage set apart as a quotation is reproduced exactly from the archive, with any cut marked by an ellipsis, and each carries its source. The chapter openings and closing lines are mine, offered only as a way into his words. Where my sentences and his seem to disagree, his are the ones to trust.

1. The Calamity

In 1967 something happened in U.G.'s body that he refused to dress in spiritual language. He described it as a death, a set of physical changes, a breakdown of the way experience had always been put together. He chose the word calamity on purpose, to keep it away from every story of bliss.

I call these events a calamity because from the point of view of one who thinks this is something fantastic, blissful, full of love, ecstasy and all that kind of a thing, this is physical torture.

The Mystique of Enlightenment, Part One

This state is a physical condition of your being. It is not some kind of psychological mutation.

The Mystique of Enlightenment

the hands and feet become cold, the body becomes stiff, the heartbeat slows down

The Natural State

My body had gone away and it has never come back. The points of contact are all that is there for the body.

The Natural State

Anything you touch is always translated as hard, soft, warm, cold, wet, dry and so on.

The Mystique of Enlightenment, Part Two

There is no such thing as experience here. …

The Mystique of Enlightenment

What he lived through was not a reward, and he never offered it as one.

2. The Search That Chokes

U.G. did not deny that people suffer or that they long for something else. He denied that the longing leads anywhere. Every spiritual goal, in his account, is a version of the wish for permanent happiness, and the effort to reach it feeds the very self it hopes to end.

I have had to search and it is the search itself which has been choking me and keeping me out of my natural state.

Mind is a Myth

transformation, moksha, and all that stuff are just variations of the same theme: permanent happiness

Thought is Your Enemy

There is no moksha, no jivanmukti and no atman. And there is no such thing as self-realization.

Stopped in Our Tracks

there is no self to realize

The Mystique of Enlightenment, Part One

…the beyond can never be experienced by you; it is when the 'you' is not there.

The Mystique of Enlightenment, Part Two

everything you are doing to be at peace with yourself is what is destroying the peace that is already there.

The Courage to Stand Alone

All your experiences, all your meditations, all your prayer, all that you do, is self-centred. … whatever you do to be free from the self also is a self-centred activity … strengthening the self, adding momentum, gathering momentum, so it is taking you in the opposite direction.

Remembering U.G. (WELL memorial compilation)

…all the positive approaches that man has invented and used for centuries have not resulted in anything useful. They have not produced the results you have been promised.

The Courage to Stand Alone, Part III

The road goes nowhere, and walking it faster only takes you further away.

3. Thought Is You

For U.G. there is no thinker behind thought. There is only thought, which is memory, which is the past applied to a present it can never catch. What we call reality, truth, time and space are, in his view, things thought has built and then mistaken for the world.

Thought is you.

Stopped in Our Tracks, Book Two

all we can know is memory and memory is thought

The Natural State

It [thought] is a mechanical thing and can solve only mechanical problems. But you want to use it to understand something living, and that is the problem.

Stopped in Our Tracks, Book Two

Thought can never capture the movement of life, it is much too slow. It is like lightning and thunder. They occur simultaneously, but sound, traveling slower than light, reaches you later, creating the illusion of two separate events.

Mind is a Myth, Ch. 3 Not Knowing Is Your Natural State

We have invented reality. Otherwise you have no way of experiencing the reality of anything.

No Way Out, Ch. 4 You Invent Your Reality

Time and space, apart from the ideas of 'time' and 'space', do not exist at all.

Mind is a Myth

There is no such thing as truth. The only thing that is actually there is your "logically" ascertained premise, which you call "truth."

Mind is a Myth, Ch. I The Certainty That Blasts Everything

You think when you don't want to do anything. Thinking is a poor alternative to acting. Your thinking is consuming all your energy. Act, don't think!

A Taste of Death

Whatever you take yourself to be, he says, is made of the same material as your thoughts.

4. A Mechanism That Protects Itself

Here U.G. is at his most severe. Thought, he says, exists to protect its own continuity, and everything it produces carries that purpose inside it. Fear is born with it. Violence is part of it. Sorrow lasts exactly as long as the thinking about sorrow.

…everything you stand for, believe in, experience and aspire to is the result of thought. And thought is destructive because it is nothing more than a protective mechanism.

Thought is Your Enemy

nothing more than a protective mechanism, programmed to protect its own interests at all costs

Thought is Your Enemy

the very birth of thought creates fear

the fear of losing what we have and what we know

Thought is Your Enemy

…thought is violent. Anything that is born out of thought is destructive.

Thought is Your Enemy

That is to say, all that is born out of thought is destructive, irrespective of its nature, whether it be religious, scientific or political.

5. No One There

If thought is the self, then the self has no existence apart from the thinking that keeps it going. U.G. took this all the way. There is no person inside, no private mind, no individual at the centre of experience. What remains when the continuity breaks is not a better self but no self at all.

there is no entity called 'self' independent of the thought process

No Way Out, Ch. 1 The Unrational Philosophy of U.G. Krishnamurti

You are not a "person", not a "thing", not a discrete entity surrounded by "other" things.

Mind is a Myth, Ch. I The Certainty That Blasts Everything

…the constant use of thought to give continuity to your separate self.

Mind is a Myth

What you call 'yourself' is fear. The 'you' is born out of fear; it lives in fear, functions in fear and dies in fear.

No Way Out

There is no such thing as your mind and my mind. … mind is everywhere, sort of like the air we breathe.

Mind is a Myth, IV

there is no individual there at all

The Natural State

Charity is selfishness.

6. The Body Goes On Alone

Against thought U.G. set the living organism. The body, for him, is intelligent, self-sufficient and wholly indifferent to spiritual ideas. It needs food, clothing and shelter. It does not fear death, because it does not die in the way the self imagines dying. It has been functioning all along without our help.

…this is the way you, stripped of the machinations of thought, are also functioning.

The Biology of Enlightenment

What is there is only the body. It is actually a computer, but we are not ready to accept it.

Thinking Allowed interview with Jeffrey Mishlove, Mind As a Myth

The body does not exist except as a thought.

Mind is a Myth

The body or organism is not interested in spiritual matters.

Science and U.G.

Each cell of a living organism cooperates with the cell next to it. … The cells stick together not out of brotherhood, love and that kind of thing, but out of the urgent drive to survive.

The pleasure of sex, for instance, is by nature temporary. The body can't take uninterrupted pleasure for long, it would be destroyed.

No Way Out

The body cannot be afraid of death. The movement that is created by society or culture is what does not want to come to an end.

No Way Out

The body changes from one state into another as recycling of matter.

in that sense the body is immortal

7. What Society Made of Us

U.G. did not separate the inner world from the outer one. The self that thought builds is the self that culture installs, and the violence of nations, religions and ideologies is the same self-protection writ large. He saw no qualitative change in human thinking and expected none.

Society or culture or whatever you might want to call it, has created us all solely and wholly for the purpose of maintaining its continuity and status quo.

Thought is Your Enemy, Ch. II Throw Away Your Crutches

no qualitative change in man's thinking

Mind is a Myth, Ch. I The Certainty That Blasts Everything

Don't forget that in the name of "Love thy neighbor as thyself" millions and millions of people have died, more than in all the recent wars put together.

Thought is Your Enemy

Political institutions and ideologies are the warty outgrowth of the religious thinking of the man; in a way responsible for the tragedy of mankind.

No Way Out

You draw lines on this planet.

No Way Out, Ch. 7 What Kind of a Human Being Do You Want?

basically violent, because thought is violent

Remembering U.G. (WELL memorial compilation)

The ideals we fight for are written by the same hand that draws the borders.

8. Don't Follow Me

U.G. would not be anyone's teacher. He dismissed every authority, his own included, and told those who came to him that nobody could give them what they wanted. What he pointed to instead was the courage to stand on one's own, which he said cannot be developed, only uncovered when the burden of the past falls away. He spoke of his own death without any comfort at all.

Don't follow me, I'm lost.

Thought is Your Enemy

A real guru, if there is one, frees you from himself.

U.G. Krishnamurti, A Life, Ch. 2 Early Years

WHAT I HAVE FOUND, OFF AND BY MYSELF, IS THAT ALL TEACHERS RELIGIOUS, SECULAR AND THOSE SCIENTISTS AND FILTHY DOCTORS ARE WRONG

You have to liberate yourself.

You can have the courage to climb the mountain, swim the lakes, go on a raft to the other side of the Atlantic or Pacific. … The courage to be on your own, to stand on your two solid feet, is something which cannot be given by somebody. You cannot free yourself of that burden by trying to develop that courage. If you are freed from the entire burden of the entire past of mankind, then what is left there is the courage.

The Courage to Stand Alone, Nice Meeting You, and Goodbye

To be yourself requires extraordinary intelligence. You are blessed with that intelligence…

The Mystique of Enlightenment, Part One

…nature had been allowed to go on in its own way, everybody would have become a unique flower.

If you have the courage to touch life for the first time, you will never know what hit you. Everything man has thought, felt and experienced is gone, and nothing is put in its place.

Afterword

It is tempting to read U.G. as one more teacher of non-duality with a rougher manner. The passages here resist that reading. He did not say the self is an illusion to be seen through. He said there is nothing to see through and no one to do the seeing, and that the attempt is itself the trouble. He did not recommend his condition. He described it as torture, as a death, as something that happened to a body and could not be chosen.

What remains, if one stays with him, is an unusual respect for the living organism and a refusal of every consolation offered in its name. He would likely have laughed at this book. He might also have let it be, since he gave his words away and asked nothing about what became of them.
