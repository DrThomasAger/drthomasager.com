Self, God and Christ in Bernadette Roberts, a Reading Guide

Customself God Christ2026-11-04 · composed with claude-opus-5-5 from the verified quotationsEdit this page

Bernadette Roberts (1931–2017) was a Catholic contemplative and former Carmelite nun who wrote of a passage beyond the union of the soul with God, in which the true self itself falls away. The six short passages below are hers, quoted exactly. The questions after each are written for this guide, as an invitation to slow reading and prayer, alone or with others. There is no need to answer them quickly, or at all. Read a passage, sit with it in silence, and let the questions open it rather than settle it.

1. Where the maps end

Our contemplative maps of the journey come to an end with the discovery of the true self and its oneness with the divine.

— What Is Self?

  • Which map of the spiritual life have you been living by, and where does it say the journey ends?
  • What would it mean for you if the true self were not the final word?
  • Can you hold a map loosely enough to let it end?

2. Self and God belong together

Without a personal, subjective self, we could not even speak of an impersonal, non-subjective God; one is just relative to the other.

— Interview with Stephan Bodian, 1986

  • When you speak of God, who is the one speaking?
  • How much of your sense of God depends on a sense of yourself as the one who knows, loves or seeks?
  • What might remain if both were no longer held apart?

3. All but the self

Truly, God is all that exists–all, of course, but the self.

— Mystics, Masters, Saints and Sages (2001), her autobiographical sketch

  • Where in your own day do you feel most like something separate from God?
  • Does this sentence feel like a loss to you, or a relief?
  • What would change in your prayer if you no longer needed to bring yourself to it?

4. The truest communication

The truest communication with God is absolute, total silence; there is not a single word in existence that can convey this communication.

— The Path to No-Self

  • What place does silence have in your prayer, beside words and images?
  • Have you known moments when words fell away and nothing seemed missing?
  • Is there a silence you avoid because it asks too much of you?

5. The death of Christ

As a Christian, I saw the no-self experience as the true nature of Christ's death

— The Christian Contemplative Journey: Essays on the Path (2017)

  • How have you usually understood the death of Christ, and what does this reading add to it or take from it?
  • What in you resists the thought that the cross concerns the end of self?
  • How might Good Friday and Easter read differently in the light of this passage?

6. Not for one person only

if the incarnation was just about one human being's oneness with God, it would be meaningless to the rest of mankind

— The Real Christ

  • Do you think of the incarnation as something that happened to Jesus alone, or as something that concerns you?
  • What would it mean for your life if the oneness of God and humanity were not reserved for one person?
  • Where do you see that oneness in the people around you?
