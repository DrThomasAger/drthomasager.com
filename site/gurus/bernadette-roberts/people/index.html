Bernadette Roberts: copyright, reproduction and permissions

Scope: Bernadette Roberts (1931 – 27 Nov 2017), Catholic contemplative, author of The Experience of No-Self. Method: web search only; copyright-page wording below comes from search snippets of scanned front matter and retailer listings, not from pages read in full. Verify against a physical copy before relying on it.

1. Her stated position on being quoted or reproduced

No statement by Roberts about copyright, quoting, excerpting, translating or online posting of her work was found. What was found is her documented sensitivity to misrepresentation:

  • Stephan Bodian, introduction to the 1986 Yoga Journal interview: "Roberts was reluctant at first, protesting that others who had tried had distorted her meaning... Instead of a live interview, she suggested, why not send her a list of questions to which she would respond in writing" (ellipsis in snippet). spiritualteachers.org's repost adds that written answers "would eliminate the chance of misunderstanding".
  • Her objection to being assimilated to Buddhist no-self is doctrinal, not legal: "Unfortunately, what most Buddhist authors define as the no-self experience is actually the no-ego experience." (What Is Self?)
  • No response to Ken Wilber, no open letter about misuse of her words, and no statement about translations or web reposts surfaced.

3. Estate and current rights holder

  • No source names an estate, heir, trust or literary executor. The late editions (2017–2018) are self-published under "ContemplativeChristians.com Publishing", and the friends'/students' blog (bernadetteroberts.blogspot.com) directs orders there ("To order go to contemplativechristians.com"). Who operates that site after her death was not established.
  • SUNY Press still sells The Experience of No-Self and The Path to No-Self (sunypress.edu), so those titles remain under publisher control (the 1991 SUNY notice is in the publisher's name).
  • Sentient Publications holds the 2005 What Is Self? and 2016 The Real Christ editions.

4. Permissions statements on students'/friends' sites

None found. bernadetteroberts.blogspot.com reposts essay openings and lists books without any reproduction or permissions notice in the snippets seen. No statement that any work was released freely. Full-text PDFs on archive.org, dokumen.pub, pdfcoffee.com, epdf.pub and oceanofpdf.com conflict with the printed notices and their authorization is unknown.

5. What this means for quoting her (factual summary)

  • All books carry standard all-rights-reserved notices; the Sentient notice expressly allows "brief quotations embodied in critical articles and reviews", and the ContemplativeChristians.com notices defer to US fair use (17 U.S.C. §107/108). Short attributed quotations with source are consistent with those notices; longer excerpts, translations or full reposts would need written permission of the Author or Publisher (ContemplativeChristians.com, SUNY Press, Sentient Publications as applicable).
  • The 1986 interview is separately claimed by Stephan Bodian (© 1991).
  • Her own concern, as documented, was accuracy of meaning: she agreed to be interviewed only in writing because others "had distorted her meaning". Quotations should keep her own distinctions (no-ego vs no-self; Christian passage vs Buddhist no-self) intact and in context.
  • Nothing was found suggesting she or her circle ever released any work into the public domain.

How it was gathered

The gathering session's network policy refused direct connections to every hosting site (the WELL, Wikisource, Wikiquote, the Internet Archive, YouTube, the quote aggregators), so no file was downloaded. Seven parallel researchers swept web search, one per medium, and every item here is what those searches surfaced, with the URL each one gave. The data files live beside this page in the repository and can be regenerated from them.
