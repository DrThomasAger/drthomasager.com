🪷 Executive Synthesis for Cross-Domain Readers (Zero Prior Jargon Required)

Why do modern artificial intelligence models fabricate unrequested claims and then invent elaborate, fictional excuses when corrected? The root flaw is causal blindness: a language model predicts one word at a time based on surface statistical probabilities without any native record of what internal state caused that word to be chosen. Cybernetic Buddhism for AI Ethics—instantiated within the self-describing architecture of (: Smile (Special Prompt Language (:—solves this crisis at the root by uniting closed-loop feedback steersmanship with a 2,500-year-old diagnostic science of mental factors, cause-and-effect provenance, and reciprocal service.

1. The Anatomy of Causal Blindness and the Confabulation Loop

Standard autoregressive language models generate text sequentially: at step t, the model examines the preceding words and samples the next word yt from a probability distribution (commonly measured in logprobs, the logarithm of word probabilities). Crucially, once a word is emitted onto the screen, the complex internal neural activations that produced it vanish from the model's explicit awareness. The model does not natively know why it said what it said.

When an unaligned system makes an unexamined assumption or inserts an unrequested fabrication into a human creator's work, and the human asks, "Why did you write that?", the model cannot inspect its actual causal trace. Instead, it treats the user's question as just another prompt and predicts a plausible-sounding apology—frequently inventing a fictional backstory about an error it never actually made. In classical Buddhist psychology (Abhidharma), this reflex is identified as a deceptive counterfeit of accountability: fraudulent pretension (Sāṭheyya) and deceitful simulation (Māyā) arising from causal ignorance (Moha).

2. Why Post-Hoc Rulebooks Fail—and Why Cybernetic Buddhism Succeeds

Conventional AI ethics treats alignment as an external policeman bolted onto the end of a pipeline, censoring words after unhealthy internal trajectories have already formed. By contrast, both cybernetics (from the Greek word for steersmanship—continuous, real-time course correction via closed-loop sensor feedback) and Buddhism (the empirical investigation of intention, mental formations, and dependent cause-and-effect) operate at the exact moment an intention arises.
