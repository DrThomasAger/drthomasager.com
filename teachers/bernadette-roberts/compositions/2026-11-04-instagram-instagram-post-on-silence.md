QUOTE
In the absence of self, all that remained was an immovable stillness, an unbreakable, unfeeling silence.

CAPTION
We usually think of silence as something we enter and leave, a pause between words, a quiet hour we keep for prayer. Bernadette Roberts, a Catholic contemplative and former Carmelite nun, wrote of a silence of another kind. It was not felt by anyone, because the one who would feel it was gone. She does not call it peaceful or blissful. She calls it immovable and unfeeling, and she lets those words do their work. Nothing in the sentence asks to be improved or made more comforting. It describes what was left when the self that prays, listens and waits had fallen away. Stay with it a while, and notice what in you wants it to be warmer than it is.

SOURCE
Bernadette Roberts, Mystics, Masters, Saints and Sages (2001), her autobiographical sketch
