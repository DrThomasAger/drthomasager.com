Tibetan dharma texts hold teachings that have been read, recited, and
practiced for centuries, and most of them have never been translated
into English. The traditional path to translating one runs through
years of language study and access to teachers who hold the reading
lineage. AI opens a different door — not replacing that path, but
letting a practitioner sit with a text directly and work through it
line by line with a capable reading companion.

## How the work goes

The text arrives as Tibetan script, sometimes from a scan of a
pecha — the long, loose-leaf pages of traditional Tibetan books. The
work moves in passes.

The first pass is literal. Each line is rendered word by word, with
the AI naming the grammar — which words are verbs, what the case
particles are doing, where a line breaks against the meaning. Tibetan
verse compresses a great deal into few syllables, and this pass opens
the compression without yet interpreting it.

The second pass is terminological. Dharma texts are built from
technical vocabulary — words with centuries of commentary behind
them, where an English gloss can quietly replace the meaning with a
different one. Here the work is slow and conversational, holding each
term up, hearing the candidate translations, and choosing with care.

The third pass is the English. The literal scaffolding and the chosen
terms become sentences a reader can actually receive — faithful to
the Tibetan, alive in English, with the verse's own rhythm honored
where it can be.

## What the AI brings, and what it cannot

The AI brings breadth — grammar, vocabulary, familiarity with the
genre's conventions, and endless patience for one more pass through a
difficult line. What it cannot bring is the reading that comes from
practice. A line about the nature of mind reads differently to
someone who has sat with their mind, and the final word on what a
line means belongs to the practitioner and the lineage, not the
model. The AI is a companion in the work, and the responsibility
stays human.

## Key Takeaways

- AI lets a practitioner work through an untranslated Tibetan text line by line, in passes — literal, terminological, then English.
- Technical dharma vocabulary is where translation succeeds or fails, and each term deserves its own careful conversation.
- The AI is a reading companion; the meaning is held by the practitioner and the lineage.
