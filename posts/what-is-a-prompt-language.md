When you write to a language model, you are already using a structure,
whether you chose one or not. Most people borrow theirs — markdown
headings from documents, XML tags from data files, bullet points from
meeting notes. The words are addressed to the model, but the structure
was designed for something else entirely.

A prompt language is a structure designed for the prompt itself. It is
a consistent way of opening sections, closing them, marking what matters
most, and carrying tone — made for the situation of one mind writing to
another.

## What the structure of a prompt does

A language model reads everything in a prompt, including the scaffolding
around the words. Delimiters, nesting, emphasis marks — all of it enters
the model's reading and conditions what comes back. This means the
structure of a prompt is not neutral packaging. It is part of the message.

A prompt language takes this seriously. Instead of letting the
scaffolding say whatever its borrowed home says — "this is a config
file", "this is documentation" — a prompt language lets the scaffolding
say what you actually mean. This section is warm. This instruction is
central. This part is an example, held apart from the instructions
around it.

## What having one changes

Writing in a prompt language changes three things.

First, consistency. When every prompt you write opens and closes its
sections the same way, the model meets a stable world across all your
prompts, and you stop re-deciding structure every time you write.

Second, graded attention. Natural writing shouts with capital letters
and bold text when something matters. A prompt language can grade
emphasis deliberately, in steps, so the most essential instruction is
visibly the most essential — to you and to the model.

Third, tone carried structurally. A prompt language can make the
delimiters themselves carry the spirit of the writing, so the prompt's
kindness or precision is present in its anatomy before a single
instruction is read.

## Key Takeaways

- Every prompt has a structure, and structure is part of the message.
- A prompt language is structure designed for prompting rather than borrowed from documents or data.
- It gives you consistency, graded attention, and tone carried in the anatomy of the prompt itself.
