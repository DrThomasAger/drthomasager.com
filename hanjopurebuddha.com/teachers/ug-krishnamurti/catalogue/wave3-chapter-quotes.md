# U.G. Krishnamurti — Wave 3: chapter-targeted quotes

Source method: web-search summaries of Wikisource / WELL full texts, grouped by book and chapter. Confidence S = book/chapter page surfaced directly; T = quote aggregator or secondary page. kind = verbatim / fragment / paraphrase. Excludes Jiddu Krishnamurti entirely.

Total quotes: 88

## Recovered chapter lists

**The Mystique of Enlightenment** (ed. Rodney Arms, 1st ed. 1982; Wikisource from well.com): Part One (autobiographical/"U.G.", conversations India & Switzerland 1973–76) · Part Two (1973 conversations compiled by Brodsky; the natural state is not a teaching) · Part Three (1980 interview by Prof. HSK, Mysore — crisis, India, the West) · Part Four (memory, thought, glands/chakras).

**Mind is a Myth** (ed. Terry Newland, Dinesh Publications, 1988): I The Certainty That Blasts Everything · II Hope Is for Tomorrow, Not Today · III Not Knowing Is Your Natural State · IV There Is Nothing to Understand · V We Have Created This Jungle Society · VI The Body as a Crucible.

**Thought is Your Enemy** (ed. Frank Noronha & J.S.R.L. Narayana Moorty, Sowmya 1991; conversations India, Switzerland, Australia, Netherlands, U.K. 1985–90; 8 chapters): 1 No Mind, No Soul, Only the Body · 2 Throw Away Your Crutches! · 3 The Robot Is Dreaming · 4 A Jolt of Lightning · 5 Love Is Only a Trump Card · 6 We Are Heading Towards Disaster · 7 The End of Illusion Is Death · 8 Lost in the Jungle.

**No Way Out** (ed. Narayana Moorty & Frank Noronha, Akshaya 1991; Wikisource Introduction + Chapters 1–11): 1 The Unrational Philosophy of U.G. Krishnamurti · 2 Nothing to Be Transformed · 3 What Is the Meaning of Life? (page header 'Sunyata') · 4 You Invent Your Reality · 5 Religious Thinking Is Responsible for Man's Tragedy (page header 'Almost Wanting Enlightenment') · 6 Seeking Strengthens Separation · 7 What Kind of Human Being Do You Want? · 8 (title not surfaced; repeats the 'what kind of human being' question) · 9 Leave the Body Alone · 10 It's Terror, Not Love That Keeps Us Together (interview with a scientist) · 11 Is He for Real? (anonymous visitor's account, Mill Valley).

**The Courage to Stand Alone** (ed. Ellen Chrystal; Amsterdam "Give Up" tapes 1982; 1st pub. 1997/2001): Part I You Don't Have to Do a Thing · Part II I Cannot Create the Hunger in You · Part III Nice Meeting You, and Goodbye.

**Stopped in Our Tracks** (K. Chandrasekhar, tr. Narayana Moorty): Book One = Stories of U.G. in India (Wikisource); Book Two = Excerpts from U.G.'s Dialogues (2005); Second & Third Series also exist.

**The Sage and the Housewife** (Shanta Kelker, Sowmya 1990; Smriti 2005, 129 pp., with glossary and a chapter on UG's teaching).

**Science and U.G.** (Wikisource; well.com reddi.htm): introductory section on the author's encounters with U.G.; Ch. 1 The Uniqueness of Man; further chapters pending.

**The Natural State** (Wikisource / well.com natural_state.htm): single essay-compilation, no chapters.

## Quotes by book and chapter

### The Mystique of Enlightenment

#### Part Four

- [S/verbatim] "There is nothing to understand — that is the understanding I am talking about."
- [S/verbatim] "I have nothing to give; you have nothing to take."

#### Part One

- [S/verbatim, 1967] "I call it 'calamity' because from the point of view of one who thinks this is something fantastic, blissful and full of beatitude, love, or ecstasy, this is physical torture." — _Describing the 1967 event; quoted in the WELL biography chapter 9._
- [T/verbatim] "Not a calamity to me but a calamity to those who have an image that something marvelous is going to happen."
- [T/verbatim, 1939] "Can you give me what you have?" — _Question U.G. put to Ramana Maharshi; Ramana: 'I can give you, but can you take it?'_
- [S/verbatim] "I am only interested in describing this state, in clearing away the occultation and mystification in which those people in the 'holy business' have shrouded the whole thing."
- [S/fragment] "I had seven years of sadhana." — _Autobiographical: between fourteen and twenty-one he visited Sivananda often and did austerities._
- [S/fragment] "my grandfather knew Blavatsky and Olcott, and then, later on, the second and third generation of Theosophists"

#### Part Three

- [S/fragment, 1980] "each individual has now the responsibility; not any particular nation — India or America or Russia"
- [S/fragment, 1980] "it has to come, and it will come from the West" — _Followed by the claim that India has no chance of being the source._

#### Part Two

- [S/paraphrase, 1973] "What I am saying is outside the field of teachability; it is simply a description of the way I am functioning." — _Search summary rendered in third person; wording close to the Wikisource text._

#### (chapter not identified)

- [T/verbatim] "It is fear that makes you believe that you are living and that you will be dead." — _Aggregators cite Mystique of Enlightenment p.83 (Sentient ed.); some cite No Way Out._

### Mind is a Myth

#### Ch. I The Certainty That Blasts Everything

- [S/verbatim, 1988] "You are not a "person", not a "thing", not a discrete entity surrounded by "other" things."
- [S/verbatim, 1988] "Your beliefs are the result of blind acceptance of authority, all secondhand stuff."

#### Ch. III Not Knowing Is Your Natural State

- [S/fragment, 1988] "is not yours; it is something which you and your ancestors have accumulated over a long time" — _Said of the knowledge a person holds._
- [S/fragment, 1988] "belief is an extension of the survival mechanism which has operated for millions of years"

#### Ch. IV There Is Nothing to Understand

- [S/verbatim, 1988] "Nature is busy creating absolutely unique individuals, whereas culture has invented a single mold to which all must conform. It is grotesque." — _QuotePark places it in Ch. 4; Wikiquote lists under Mind is a Myth (1987)._
- [S/paraphrase, 1988] "the idea of selflessness is a protective invention against one's actual selfishness" — _Summary of the chapter's opening answer._

#### Ch. V We Have Created This Jungle Society

- [S/verbatim, 1988] "The understanding that there is nothing to understand is all there is."
- [S/fragment, 1988] "the very birth of thought creates fear"

#### (chapter not identified)

- [T/fragment, 1988] "it is the ones who believe in God, who preach peace and talk of love, who have created the human jungle"

### Thought is Your Enemy

#### (chapter not identified)

- [T/verbatim] "Political institutions and ideologies are the warty outgrowth of the religious thinking of the man; in a way responsible for the tragedy of mankind."
- [S/verbatim] "Yes it is. Because thought is violent. Anything that is born out of thought is destructive." — _Answer to a question whether thought is violent (holybooks PDF of the book)._
- [S/verbatim] "Don't forget that in the name of "Love thy neighbor as thyself" millions and millions of people have died, more than in all the recent wars put together."

### No Way Out

#### Ch. 1 The Unrational Philosophy of U.G. Krishnamurti

- [S/fragment, 1991] "there is no entity called 'self' independent of the thought process"
- [T/verbatim, 1991] "I am not anti-rational, just unrational. You may infer a rational meaning in what I say or do, but it is your doing, not mine." — _Wikiquote cites No Way Out; Dharmapedia cites Stopped in Our Tracks._

#### Ch. 10 It's Terror, Not Love That Keeps Us Together

- [S/fragment, 1991] "the term 'natural state' is not a synonym for enlightenment, freedom, or God-realization" — _Also quoted in a Medium summary of The Natural State._

#### Ch. 3 What Is the Meaning of Life?

- [S/fragment, 1991] "anything that is born out of thought is destructive in its nature" — _Chapter 6 (Seeking Strengthens Separation) has the variant 'any action that is born out of thinking is destructive in its nature'._

#### Ch. 4 You Invent Your Reality

- [S/verbatim, 1991] "We have invented reality. Otherwise you have no way of experiencing the reality of anything."

#### Ch. 8

- [S/verbatim, 1991] "The basic question which we have to ask for ourselves is: what kind of a human being do you want? But unfortunately we have placed before ourselves the model of a perfect being." — _Wikisource shows this wording in Chapter 8; Chapter 4 has the same question in other words._

#### Ch. 9 Leave the Body Alone

- [S/paraphrase, 1991] "I don't exercise; I used to walk a great deal." — _Reply to a questioner noting he does no yoga or exercises._

#### (chapter not identified)

- [T/verbatim, 1991] "Charity is selfishness."
- [T/fragment, 1991] "man has created religion because it gives him a cover"
- [T/verbatim, 1991] "Transformation, moksha, liberation, and all that stuff are just variations on the same theme: permanent happiness."

### The Courage to Stand Alone

#### Part I You Don't Have to Do a Thing

- [S/verbatim, 1982] "You don't have to do a thing." — _Title line of Part I; variant on inner-quest: 'to be yourself is very easy, you don't have to do a thing'._

#### Part III Nice Meeting You, and Goodbye

- [S/verbatim, 1982] "That any fool can do, but the courage to be on your own, to stand on your two solid feet, is something which cannot be given by somebody." — _Preceded by: 'You can have the courage to climb the mountain, swim the lakes, go on a raft to the other side of the Atlantic or Pacific...'_

### The Natural State

#### (chapter not identified)

- [S/verbatim] "My body had gone away and it has never come back. The points of contact are all that is there for the body."
- [S/verbatim] "Anything you touch is always translated as hard, soft, warm, cold, wet, dry and so on."
- [S/verbatim] "in the 'natural state' the continuity of thought is absent. What is present is a disjoined series of independent interactions."
- [S/fragment] "the hands and feet become cold, the body becomes stiff, the heartbeat slows down" — _Describing the periodic death-like state of the body._
- [S/fragment] "all we can know is memory and memory is thought"
- [S/paraphrase] "The natural state is not a thoughtless state; that is a hoax that has been carried on for thousands of years. You will never be without thought." — _Search summary renders in indirect speech; the Wikisource text carries this claim._
- [T/verbatim] "The knowledge about it, all that he has learned, is held in the background, unless there is a demand." — _Third-person description of the natural state quoted on Jhana 8._
- [S/paraphrase] "It is a physiological state, with no religious or mystical content."

### Stopped in Our Tracks

#### Book One (Stories of U.G. in India)

- [T/paraphrase] "I am neither a jivanmukta nor an evolved being." — _Kwize excerpts: U.G. repeatedly denies being a liberated soul or an evolved being._

#### Book Two (Excerpts from U.G.'s Dialogues)

- [S/verbatim] "It [thought] is a mechanical thing and can solve only mechanical problems."
- [S/paraphrase] "I am not suggesting that you should stop thinking; thought is what you are." — _Rendered indirectly in the search summary of well.com/chandra.htm._

#### (chapter not identified)

- [S/fragment] "man, who supposedly has achieved so much progress in the scientific field, cannot even predict precisely when an earthquake is going to occur"

### The Sage and the Housewife

#### Publisher's Note

- [S/verbatim, 1990] "Neither the subject nor the author is famous." — _U.G.'s reaction to the idea of the book, recorded in the publisher's note._

### Science and U.G.

#### (chapter not identified)

- [S/verbatim] "That is to say, all that is born out of thought is destructive, irrespective of its nature, whether it be religious, scientific or political."
- [S/verbatim] "The body or organism is not interested in spiritual matters."
- [S/verbatim] "This can also lead to self-destruction for the sake of even a silly idea, as witnessed in wars."
- [S/verbatim] "The body changes from one state into another as recycling of matter." — _Wikisource text; preceded by 'So also are the birth and death of thought.'_
