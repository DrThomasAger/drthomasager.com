Subject: U.G. Krishnamurti (Uppaluri Gopala Krishnamurti, 1918–2007). Jiddu Krishnamurti material was excluded throughout (several search hits for "Saanen", "sorrow" and "Limits of Thought" were J.K. items and were discarded).

Method: WebSearch only (about 65 queries). ## 2. Quotes

Legend: S = wording seen verbatim in a source snippet; T = wording reconstructed or secondary. Kind: verbatim / fragment / paraphrase.

### Stopped in Our Tracks (K. Chandrasekhar; Book Two = "Excerpts from U.G.'s Dialogues", compiled from audio tapes, ed. J.S.R.L. Narayana Moorty, 2005; well.com/user/jct/chandra.htm)

1. "It [thought] is a mechanical thing and can solve only mechanical problems. But you want to use it to understand something living, and that is the problem." — Book Two, section "Thinking Cannot Solve Your Problems". S / verbatim (first two sentences). One quote site attributes it to Mahesh Bhatt's *A Taste of Death*.
2. "Food, clothing and shelter — these are the basic needs." — Book Two, section "Basic Needs"; the passage ends with a warning about self-deception. S / fragment. (ru.citaty.net says No Way Out ch. 4.)
3. "Tell them that there is nothing to understand." — attributed to Book Two in a reader review; TOC has "There Is Nothing to Do". T / fragment.
4. "There is no moksha, no jivanmukti and no atman. And there is no such thing as self-realization." — chapter of the same name, **Book One** (Stories of U.G. in India). S / verbatim.
5. "Why do you call it green?" — Book One tree anecdote. T / paraphrase.
6. "I am myself a thief." — Book One anecdote about burglaries. T / paraphrase.
7. "My aim is to silence you." — Book Two passage on his approach (TOC also has "Samadhi and Ultimate Samadhi"). T / paraphrase.

### The Biology of Enlightenment (ed. Mukunda Rao; conversations 1967–71 in Saanen, Paris, Italy, India; HarperCollins India 2010, 430 pp.; launched Crossword, Residency Road, Bangalore, 8 Jan 2011 by Mahesh Bhatt with Frank Noronha and Chandrasekhar Babu — sulochanosho report; reissued 2015, 448 pp.)

8. "How I got into this state I will never be able to tell." — followed by: his autobiography ends on 13 August 1967. S / verbatim (Reality Sandwich excerpt).
9. "It is not a change in the structure of your thinking, but a change in the structure of the whole of your physical being." S / fragment.
10. "Whatever transformation I have undergone is within the structure of the human body and not in the mind at all." T / paraphrase (Rao's intro, reported speech).
11. "I am not trying to sell anything here." — Paris 1967 context; may be from *Mystique of Enlightenment*. T / fragment.
12. Rao (editor, not U.G.): the natural state is "a pure and simple physical and physiological state of being", where "not the self or the mind, but a different kind of intelligence" operates. T / paraphrase, context only.

### The Sage and the Housewife (Shanta Kelker; Sowmya Publishers, Bangalore, Nov 1990; Smriti Books 2005; copyright released; well.com sage1.htm–sage7.htm, Wikisource)

13. "No one escapes the natural laws." — ch. 1 "Stories of U.G. in Bangalore", on detaching from pain. S / fragment.
14. "It is the body that is immortal. It is living from moment to moment." (…people become rotten through culture) — Wikiquote: ch. 3. T / fragment; Quotepark says No Way Out ch. 4.
15. "I don't know what happiness is." — ch. 4, reply to a visitor asking if he is always happy. T / paraphrase.
16. "A person to whom Kundalini rises would dismiss the very concept of Kundalini." — ch. 7 (Nov 1988 Bangalore). T / paraphrase.
17. "My teaching, if that is the word you want to use, has no copyright. You are free to reproduce, distribute, interpret, misinterpret, distort, garble, do what you like, even claim authorship, without my consent or the permission of anybody." — standard disclaimer in the front matter, cited in reviews of this book. T / verbatim.

Chapter texts of sage2, sage3, sage5, sage6 were not reached by search snippets; a direct read of the WELL/Wikisource pages is the next step for more verbatim lines.
