## The Mystique of Enlightenment

- “It is fear that makes you believe that you are living and that you will be dead. What we do not want is the fear to come to an end.”

## Attribution conflicts and warnings

- **Jiddu vs U.G.**: searches on love, yoga, happiness, questioning, Indian/American culture and Theosophy returned kfoundation.org / krishnamurti.org / scienceandnonduality / thebacainstitute pages — all Jiddu Krishnamurti. None were harvested. The Theosophy query returned only J.K. material; no U.G. statement on Besant/Leadbeater was found.
- **'The body cannot be afraid of death…'**: Goodreads credits the same passage to both *Mind Is a Myth* and *No Way Out*.
- **'Thinking is a poor alternative to acting' / 'Act, don't think!'**: Wikiquote attributes these to Mahesh Bhatt's book (which it labels 'A Taste of Death', 1995 — probably *U.G. Krishnamurti: A Life*, 1992) and warns that Bhatt's framing is often misquoted as U.G.'s own words.
- **'It is fear that makes you believe that you are living…'**: credited to *The Mystique of Enlightenment* (one aggregator: p. 83) but circulates with variant wording ('new science' / 'new sciences').
- **'What is morality?…'**: one blog version drops the clause about the faculty of distinguishing right from wrong.
- **'Charity is the filthiest invention…' / 'Charity is selfishness'**: aggregator and blog only; not found in the *Thought Is Your Enemy* text although often shelved there.
- **'There is no power outside of man. Man has created God out of fear…'**: Wikiquote places it in *Mystique* (1982); one search attributed the opening to *The Courage to Stand Alone*.
- **'We are not created for any grander purpose…'**: Wikisource ch. 4 reads 'you', aggregators 'we/us'.
- **'I have nothing to say and what I am saying is not born out of my thinking'**: from an undated transcribed conversation in Bhatt's biography; the 1972 Bangalore 'First and Last Public Talk' could not be confirmed as its venue.
- **Society / Certainty anthologies** (Sunita Pant Bansal, ed.): the title lines ('It's terror, not love that keeps us together'; 'Life has no beginning, no end') are editor-chosen headings assembled from U.G.'s talks, not independently dated utterances.
- **Swan Song**: all copies agree it was dictated to Louis Brawley in mid-February 2007; the search layer would not return long verbatim runs, so only the opening sentence is verbatim and the rest is paraphrase.
- **Ramana anecdote**: 'Can you give me what you have?' / 'I can give you, but can you take it?' is U.G.'s own retelling (Mystique Part One), reproduced only via blogs here.
- **Food, doctors, children**: the cookbook PDF, inner-quest and the WELL biography are interview or reported-speech accounts, marked T.
